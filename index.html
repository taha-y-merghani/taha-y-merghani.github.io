A guided archive of formation

I learned to read systems before I had power inside them.

A guided archive of the experiences, artifacts, and questions that shaped how I think about language, AI, institutions, public trust, and human judgment.

Read this as a museum of formation, not a portfolio.

I have been shaped by exams, languages, migration, research labs, product teams, public writing, retail floors, and institutions that did not always know how to read me back. The archive below is organized around what those settings taught me to notice.

The recurring question is simple: what does pressure reveal about a system that the system cannot say about itself?

How to read the rooms

Each room is built around four questions: what happened, what I noticed, what I made, and what it taught me about systems. The artifacts are evidence, not decoration.

Six rooms

A sequence of pressure, language, institutions, and artifacts.

These are not permanent identity buckets. They are rooms in an archive: places where a particular kind of perception was trained.

Room 1

The Exam / Hidden Structure

Long before I had institutional access, I learned that authority has structure. Exams, rankings, schools, borders, and admissions systems all presented themselves as fixed. I learned to study them as systems.

Artifact: Sudan national exam story Read essay
What happened

I studied a national exam under pressure and learned to see the hidden structure inside the scoring system.

What I noticed

Authority often appears as judgment, but it is also procedure, pattern, incentive, and constraint.

What I made

A prediction, then later a public essay about the pattern-recognition behind it.

What it taught

Systems become less mystical when you can model how they make decisions.

Room 2

Translation / Language as Survival

Language was not only a research topic. It was how I learned to read people, class, intimacy, danger, authority, and belonging. Arabic schooling, English acquisition, immigration, and later NLP work all live on that line.

Artifact: Sudan to Silicon Valley Read essay
What happened

I moved across languages and institutions before I had stable footing inside either.

What I noticed

Language carries status, safety, intimacy, exclusion, and permission.

What I made

A technical path through speech, NLP, writing, and public explanation.

What it taught

Language systems are never just symbolic. They carry social consequence.

Room 3

Speech / Low-Resource Reality

At MIT CSAIL, Egyptian Arabic speech recognition made the technical problem concrete: language technology works unevenly when data, dialect, and infrastructure are uneven.

Artifact: Egyptian Arabic ASR View paper
What happened

I worked on automatic speech recognition for Egyptian Arabic using Kaldi and language resources.

What I noticed

Technical systems degrade where the world has not supplied clean data or institutional attention.

What I made

A research artifact around low-resource speech recognition and dialect-specific modeling.

What it taught

Robust AI begins with respect for the conditions under which language is actually used.

Room 4

Variation / What Models Miss

At Georgia Tech, I worked on social media language variation and model errors. The lesson has stayed with me: language technology does not fail evenly across communities, styles, and networks.

Artifact: Stylistic Variation in Social Media POS Tagging View ACL paper
What happened

I co-authored work with Murali Balusu and Jacob Eisenstein on social network structure and POS tagger errors.

What I noticed

Errors are not only technical failures. They can trace social structure.

What I made

An ACL Anthology paper connecting stylistic variation, networks, and evaluation.

What it taught

Evaluation must ask who is being misread, not only how often a system is wrong.

Room 5

Product / Language at Scale

At Apple Siri, language became a product-quality problem. The work was not just model behavior. It was tooling, localization, QA, bug workflows, and the coordination needed to make language systems legible to the people maintaining them.

Artifact: Siri QA and localization tooling View project context
What happened

I built internal tooling for multilingual NLU debugging and QA workflows.

What I noticed

Large language products need tools that help humans find where the system broke.

What I made

Python tooling and structured bug workflows for engineers and program managers.

What it taught

AI reliability is also an organizational interface problem.

Room 6

Public Consequence / Dignity Under Misrecognition

Retail floors, immigration systems, public writing, and AI deployment all sharpened one question: what happens when institutions summarize a person or workflow too quickly?

Artifact: Public essays and field notes Browse writing
What happened

I worked close to brittle workflows, public interpretation, and institutions that rely on simplified categories.

What I noticed

Misrecognition is not abstract. It changes what people are allowed to become inside a system.

What I made

Essays, prototypes, deployment notes, and a founder thesis around source-grounded judgment.

What it taught

The humane technical question is often: what should the system refuse to flatten?

Now

Current work, current ask.

The current technical direction is an advisor-grade research interface: a tool that separates source retrieval, claim verification, counterargument discovery, assumption tracking, and interpretation. The goal is not to replace the user's understanding. The goal is to preserve and sharpen it.

Founder thesis

High-quality research and AI product-building are bottlenecked by human judgment, source-grounded verification, and the ability to preserve a user's understanding rather than replacing it.
