Collaborative story generation

Mark Riedl’s lab

I worked on a storytelling agent designed to co-write stories with people. Building on Lara J. Martin and colleagues’ approach, I helped train a sequence-to-sequence baseline that turned structured plot events into sentences. I also evaluated changes to the training corpora and VerbNet rules.

Stylistic Variation in Social Media Part-of-Speech Tagging

Murali Raghu Babu Balusu, Taha Merghani, and Jacob Eisenstein

Tagging errors clustered in the social network. Sampling training data across the network helped, while a mixture-of-experts model built around network regions yielded no improvement.

Research posterMIT CSAIL · 2015

Lexical Modeling of Egyptian Arabic for Automatic Speech Recognition

Taha Merghani, Tuka Al Hanai, and James Glass

We compared pronunciation dictionaries and acoustic models using 10 hours of training speech. With our best acoustic model, the dictionary based on normalized spelling produced slightly fewer word errors than the automatically diacritized dictionary.

02 / PhD research interests

What does an evaluation miss?

In story generation, a fluent sentence could lose the meaning of the plot event it was supposed to express. In retail, an inventory record could miss what experienced workers did to recover when the shelves and the system disagreed. Both experiences shape my interest in responsible AI, especially what audits measure and whose work they capture.

Who experiences the errors?

I want to audit how errors and their consequences vary across groups, including cases where average performance looks acceptable.

Who gets to intervene?

I’m interested in how institutions make AI-assisted decisions accountable to the people affected by them. Who can contest a decision, and how do corrections change the outcomes an audit sees?
