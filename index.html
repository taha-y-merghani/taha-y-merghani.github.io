The thesis

High-quality research is not just information retrieval. It is judgment under uncertainty.

When I use AI for research, the failure mode I notice is often not that the model knows nothing. It is that it silently chooses the wrong role. It summarizes when I need sources. It recommends when I need verification. It interprets when I need the claim left intact.

The product opportunity I care about is an advisor-grade research interface: a system that helps a person verify, challenge, and extend claims while keeping the human in control of the reasoning.

What I’m exploring

  • Claim-to-source mapping
  • Counter-source discovery
  • Assumption tracking
  • Citation-grounded synthesis
  • Separation between what the source says and what the model infers
  • Interfaces for researchers, founders, analysts, and serious readers
Current build direction

What I’m building toward

I’m currently turning this thesis into small prototypes and public artifacts. The starting point is simple: take a claim, retrieve the best source material around it, surface the strongest challenges, and make the model’s interpretation auditable.

Claim verification

Tools that ask what exactly is being claimed, where the claim comes from, and what would make it weaker or false.

Source-grounded research assistance

Interfaces that prioritize source retrieval and evidence quality before synthesis or recommendation.

Human-in-the-loop judgment interfaces

Systems that expose assumptions and uncertainty so the human can keep doing the reasoning work that matters.

AI for messy real-world workflows

Practical tools for places where data is incomplete, institutions are constrained, and exception-handling is normal.
