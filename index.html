The wedge

Language technology does not fail evenly.

Language technology fails along lines of style, community, dialect, network, and who shows up in the training data. I first worked on this problem in 2018, when I co-authored “Stylistic Variation in Social Media Part-of-Speech Tagging” with Murali Balusu and Jacob Eisenstein, published in the ACL Anthology. That question is more urgent in the era of large language models, not less.

Why ACL

Why ACL 2026?

ACL is the flagship conference for NLP and language AI. I want to be in the room with people working on the consequences: evaluation, labor, education, institutions, multilingual systems, and who gets served or missed by these systems.

Papers are online. Proximity is not.

The field is there

ACL is the central gathering for NLP and language AI.

The people are there

Researchers, engineers, founders, hiring managers, and collaborators are concentrated in one place.

The questions are there

Evaluation, multilingual systems, language variation, labor, education, institutions, and consequences.

The informal layer matters

Useful opportunities form around posters, workshops, hallway conversations, and introductions.

FDE bridge

Why this connects to Forward Deployed Engineering

I’m not interested in AI as a clean benchmark artifact. I’m interested in AI systems that survive contact with reality. Real deployment means messy users, multilingual settings, unclear workflows, institutional constraints, uneven data, and consequences that do not show up in a leaderboard.

That is why my website is organized around Forward Deployed Engineering. The work I want to do sits between models, users, workflows, evaluation, and the society those systems enter.
