About

I started in language technology before the current LLM wave: Arabic speech recognition at MIT CSAIL, multilingual debugging tools at Apple Siri, and NLP research at Georgia Tech. Since then I've worked across customer analytics pipelines, computer vision in enterprise field environments, and local LLM systems. I'm now focused on applied AI deployment: building systems that survive contact with real workflows, messy data, and institutional constraints.

My path is unusual. I grew up in Sudan, ranked first nationally on university entrance exams among 400,000 students, studied computer engineering at Jackson State University (4.0 GPA), then did an M.S. at Georgia Tech focused on ML and NLP. After nine years navigating the U.S. immigration system, my asylum case was approved in September 2025. That experience shapes how I think about uncertainty, institutional constraints, and what it means to build something durable.

I write about what I learn: AI systems, deployment friction, human skill, and the gap between research and practice. Some of that writing has found an audience -- one essay reached 200K views, another was shared by Jeff Dean. I write because it forces clarity. Explaining something well is how I know I actually understand it.
